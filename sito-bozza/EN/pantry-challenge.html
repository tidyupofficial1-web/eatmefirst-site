The Pantry Challenge · Ages 10 and up

“Mum will take care of it!”

It’s something you hear at home when it’s time to do the shopping. Today Mum takes care of it. In a few years, those same children will be planning meals and buying groceries for themselves or their own families.

When you first live on your own or with a partner, deciding what to buy and cook every day can take you by surprise. It’s easy to fall back on ready meals, spend more than planned or buy things you already have. Meanwhile, something goes past its date at the back of the fridge.

That’s why we wanted to help parents get their children involved early. We call it a little game because you can start together with three things you already have at home. Checking what’s there, planning a meal and writing down what’s missing are simple habits. When it’s their turn to run a household, they’ll know where to start.

Try the free game

From the game to real grocery shopping

  1. Practise together. Open the game and try adding an item and recording what you use. Then pick three real items at home and note where they’re stored, how much is left and any date on the label.
  2. Make a shopping list. Choose a meal to make together. Ask whoever is looking after the pantry to check the ingredients, list what’s missing and suggest something to use soon. Tick off each item as it goes into the trolley.
  3. Record what you buy and use. Add the new groceries when you get home. If you use an entire jar, remove it from the inventory. If you use half a packet, update the amount left.
  4. Make it a household job. Help the children the first few times. Before the next shop, let them check the cupboards and suggest a list. Go over it together before you leave.

Invite a few friends to try the pantry challenge

Invite some of your children’s friends over and put a few food packages on the table. With an adult nearby, they can use the actual app to build a small practice pantry. Take turns reading the label, choosing where to store the product and adding it to the app.

Ask which aisle they would look in, such as dairy or fresh produce, and where they would keep each item at home. Yoghurt goes in the fridge, while a packet of pasta can go in a cupboard. Look at the labels together. A “use by” date is about food safety. A “best before” date is about quality when the food has been stored as directed.

Finally, pretend you’re making a snack. Have the children record what they used and make a list for the next shop. Before adding anything, check whether you already have it.

The website has step-by-step guides to every feature as well as the game. In the app, each field has help explaining what to enter.

Why we made the little game

We first built EatMeFirst to help families manage groceries, from putting them away to using them up and making the next shopping list. Then we realised these were skills worth passing on to children. So we made the little game to let them practise before taking care of the real pantry.

Next time you get back from the supermarket, give the children a few items to look after. Let them add each one in the app, decide where it goes and read and record the date. When you use it, have them update the amount left. Next time, ask them to check what’s missing and make the shopping list. Start together, then let them take on more of it themselves.

We wanted families to be able to practise and manage their pantry for free. The game, inventory, tracking what you use, shopping list and automatic saving on your phone are all free. Open it in your browser with no installation or sign-up. The shopping and food use data you enter stay in your device’s browser. EatMeFirst does not send them to its servers or share them with third parties.
